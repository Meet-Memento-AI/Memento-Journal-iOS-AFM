REVIEW NOTES — Memento 1.0

1. NO ACCOUNT, NO DEMO CREDENTIALS

No sign-in, account or login screen; every feature is reachable after
install, so the Sign-In fields are blank deliberately. Not applicable: no
registration, login or account-deletion flow, as no account exists
(Settings > Delete Everything erases all app data); no shared user
content; no purchases.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE

Memento is a private journal that answers questions about what you wrote.
Speak or type an entry; it is transcribed, filed and indexed on the device.
Later you ask your archive a question and get an answer from your own
entries, citing the ones used. The problem it solves: journals go unread,
and search finds only the words you happened to use.

For adults who journal and want it private. An archivist, not an advisor:
it reports what you wrote, never advising, diagnosing or using clinical
language. Not a health or medical product.

3. SETUP AND ACCESS

No credentials needed. Onboarding offers an optional app lock; "Skip for
now" bypasses it, and if enabled the lock screen has a device-passcode
fallback, so no PIN is needed from us.

The companion answers from entries; a fresh install has none:

  Journal > initials button top left (VoiceOver: "Menu") > Profile >
  Settings > "Load Sample Entries"

Clearly fictional, reversible from the same row. Then:

- Ask: message icon top right, or swipe left. Ask "what have I been
  writing about?" and tap a citation to open that entry.
- Capture: compose button, allow Microphone and Speech Recognition, speak.
- Export: Settings > "Export Your Journal".

Every permission is optional, and the app does not track you.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES

On device, Apple frameworks only: Foundation Models for all companion text,
Speech, NaturalLanguage, Vision, LocalAuthentication, Core Spotlight (off
by default), and a bundled Core ML read-aloud voice, never downloaded.
Reaching Apple: MapKit reverse geocoding to name a place when asked; your
own private CloudKit database, which syncs the journal to your other
devices if you use iCloud and which we cannot access; and Apple's speech
models on first use.

One non-Apple service: Supabase, hosting a write-only database for
volunteered answer-quality feedback. A Report sends that question and
answer; thumbs ratings need the off-by-default Settings switch "Share
Quality Feedback". Journal entries and chat history are not sent.

No authentication provider, payment processor, analytics or crash SDK,
advertising, third-party AI, or third-party Swift package.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES

The binary is identical everywhere. Nothing in it reads the device's
country or gates a feature by territory; the interface is English only.
Two things vary by device, not region: the companion needs Apple
Intelligence, and dictation needs Apple's on-device speech model for the
device language. Without either, the app says so and the rest still works.

The crisis-support card is identical in every region: the US 988 line and
a link to the International Association for Suicide Prevention's worldwide
directory.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY AND THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL

Memento is a journaling and personal-productivity app, not a health or
medical one, and provides no regulated service, so no professional
credential applies. The non-advisory design is enforced in code, by an
on-device safety layer and CI evaluation gates.

A crisis message shows a static bundled resource card: a pointer to help,
never generated, naming organisations in plain text with no logos or
claimed affiliation, beside a line saying this is not a crisis service.
