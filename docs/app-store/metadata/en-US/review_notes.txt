REVIEW NOTES — Memento 1.0

1. NO ACCOUNT, NO DEMO CREDENTIALS

No sign-in, account or login screen; every feature is reachable after
install, so the Sign-In fields are blank deliberately. Not applicable: no
registration, login or account-deletion flow and no shared user content,
as no account exists (Settings > Delete Everything erases all data).

One auto-renewing subscription, Memento Pro, annual or monthly; the
paywall shows the regional price. Journalling is free and unlimited —
writing, reading, search and export are never gated. Pro lifts the free
tier's 10-messages-a-day companion limit and unlocks Weekly reflections
and Patterns.

To see the paywall without buying: send 10 companion messages in a day, or
open Journal > Insights > Weekly. Restore Purchases is on that screen and
in Settings. Crisis messages are never limited.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE

A private journal that answers questions about what you wrote. Speak or
type an entry; it is transcribed, filed and indexed on the device. Later
you ask your archive a question and get an answer from your own entries,
citing the ones used. The problem it solves: journals go unread, and
search finds only the words you happened to use.

For adults who journal and want it private. An archivist, not an advisor
(see 6).

3. SETUP AND ACCESS

No credentials needed. Onboarding offers an optional app lock; "Skip for
now" bypasses it, and the lock screen has a device-passcode fallback, so
no PIN is needed from us.

The companion answers from entries; a fresh install has none:

  Journal > initials button top left (VoiceOver: "Menu") > Profile >
  Settings > "Load Sample Entries"

Clearly fictional, reversible from the same row. Then: Ask via the message
icon top right or swipe left ("what have I been writing about?", then tap
a citation to open that entry); Capture via the compose button, allowing
Microphone and Speech Recognition; Export at Settings > "Export Your
Journal". Permissions are all optional.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES

On device, Apple frameworks only: Foundation Models for all companion
text, Speech, NaturalLanguage, Vision, LocalAuthentication, Core Spotlight
(off by default), and a bundled Core ML voice, never downloaded.

Reaching Apple: MapKit reverse geocoding to name a place when asked; your
own private CloudKit database, which we cannot access; Apple's speech
models on first use;
and the App Store, which processes every purchase.

Two non-Apple services. Supabase hosts a write-only database for
volunteered answer-quality feedback. RevenueCat reads subscription status
only, keyed to an anonymous identifier. Journal entries and chat history
go to neither. No auth provider, analytics or crash SDK, ads, or
third-party AI.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES

The binary is identical everywhere. Nothing in it reads the device's
country or gates a feature by territory; the interface is English only,
and the App Store shows prices in local currency. Two things vary by
device, not region: the companion needs Apple Intelligence, and dictation
needs Apple's on-device speech model for the device language. Without
either the app says so, and the rest still works. The crisis card is
identical in every region.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY AND THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL

A journaling and personal-productivity app, not a health or medical one,
providing no regulated service, so no professional credential applies.
The non-advisory design is enforced in code by a safety layer and CI gates.

A crisis message shows a static bundled card, never generated: the US 988
line and the IASP directory, in plain text, no logos, beside a line saying
this is not a crisis service.
