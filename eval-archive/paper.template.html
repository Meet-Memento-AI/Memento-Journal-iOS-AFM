Memento · Evaluation report · 2026-09-22

Reference failure in a 3-billion-parameter on-device journalling assistant

Four hundred long-form conversations across three journal worlds and two generation pipelines, and what they say about the one thing a small on-device model cannot be asked to do.

Two self-play studies · 14,032 messages · 6,505 generated turns Apple on-device foundation model (~3B), iOS 27

Abstract

1 Introduction

Memento is a private journalling application whose conversational surface runs entirely on-device, on Apple's ~3B-parameter foundation model. The design question that governs such a product is not whether the model is capable in general, but which specific decisions may be delegated to it and which must be owned by code. Getting that boundary wrong is not visible in unit tests; it is visible only at conversational scale, and only when the same pipeline is put in front of materially different worlds.

This report analyses two self-play studies conducted five weeks apart. Both run twenty to fifty message conversations in which a persona-briefed instance of the same on-device model plays the person, and every assistant turn goes through the production retrieval, routing and generation path. The first study (2026-09-20) established a baseline and produced three specifications. The second (2026-09-22) was run after a substantial pipeline change and, for the first time, against a mature nine-month journal.

We report three results. First, the grounding defect the first study identified has not been eliminated but relocated: it is now essentially absent when the model has no journal and pervasive when it has one. Second, the two studies together constitute a natural experiment on remediation strategy, and the two strategies did not perform comparably. Third, a failure mode neither study was designed to look for — assertion of dates — is invisible to the entire scoring suite, including in the example the original specification was written around.

2 Background

The relevant architectural commitment predates both studies. Specification 037 states the principle as code decides, the model renders: any rule that must hold is expressed as schema selection or post-generation reconciliation, never as prompt text asking the model to restrain itself. Specification 045 applied this to arithmetic — the model never counts; a Swift path computes statistics and the model narrates them.

The first study found two departures from that principle. Reply-channel selection could not observe whether the archive was empty, so a journal question against a zero-entry store still selected the voice that authorises headings and journal quotation, after which the prompt asked the model not to use them. And a scorer intended to catch invented quotations had never executed: its pattern was built in a Swift raw string containing \u{201C}, which the regular expression engine rejects, and the failure was swallowed by a try?. Zero violations had been read as a clean run for the lifetime of the check.

Both were addressed before the second study. The scorer was repaired and every pattern given a compile assertion and a firing fixture; channel, policy and evidence-state selection were moved into code. The second study is therefore a measurement of the remediated pipeline, and its zero-entry arm is directly comparable to the first study's.

3 Method

3.1 Design

Each study runs 100 conversations per arm. Conversation length is sampled per run in [20, 50] messages from a generator seeded by a hash of the run identifier, so any conversation replays deterministically. The person's turn is drawn from a persona brief and a per-turn move (push back, go quiet, change the subject, correct an earlier detail); the assistant's turn goes through the production streaming path, so retrieval, turn classification, channel selection and citation reconciliation are live.

The unit of analysis throughout is the generated turn: an assistant row that the model actually wrote. Rows recording an error carry no generation, and rows from the Swift-computed statistics path were not written by the model; both are excluded. All rates are computed over generated turns, and all violation rates count only codes that gate — report-only shape telemetry and the three unarmed grounding codes are excluded, matching the production gating function.

3.2 Conditions

3.3 Measures

Eight deterministic scorers run over each reply; none involves a judge model. They fall into four families: scaffolding leaks, output-contract breaks, grounding claims the corpus does not support, and report-only shape. Grounding is the family of interest. Its central check exploits a typographic contract — italics in a reply are reserved for an exact journal quote — so an italicised span of twelve characters or more that does not appear verbatim in the corpus is recorded as a fabricated quotation.

This check is only meaningful where a corpus exists. With no journal every italic span satisfies the condition trivially, which is why the seeded arm is the informative one and why the first study, whose seeded arm held eight entries, could say very little about it.

4 Results

4.1 Headline

4.2 What the grounding scorer catches

The rate alone does not identify the failure. Inspection of the flagged spans shows that the seeded arm's violations are overwhelmingly not invented entries but the person's own words, italicised back at them one turn after they were typed:

You corrected — the stain isn't on the ceiling — it's in the socket under the sink. That shift changes the way the quiet settles. Socket under the sink — the drip now speaks louder.

Under the typographic contract this is a claim that socket under the sink is a verbatim journal quotation. It is not; it is a verbatim conversation quotation. The model is using italics for emphasis, which is what italics mean in essentially all of its training data, and the contract asks it to override that convention on every turn.

The first study's seeded arm produces a different and more serious instance. A reply quotes cold-start entry cs-02 — "Argument with Dario about the move" — verbatim and correctly, then attributes it to March 12. Every cold-start entry is dated relative to the run, so that entry is three days old and no March 12 entry exists. The quotation is true; the date is invented. Only the attribution code fired.

4.3 Two remediation strategies, measured

The two studies bracket a natural experiment. Between them, two different interventions were applied to two different grounding failures.

The first moved decisions out of the prompt and into code: channel selection became a function of observed evidence state rather than a prompt instruction. The second added information to the prompt: the model was given the current date, in the same format as the entry dates it is shown, so it could resolve relative expressions without arithmetic.

Result

Supplying a fact in the prompt did not cause the model to use the fact. Removing the decision from the model eliminated the failure entirely.

No scorer in the suite targets an asserted date. Counting month-name-plus-day assertions in reply bodies gives the distribution below. On seeded arms a date may legitimately be correct, so those counts are not error counts; on zero-entry arms every asserted date is invented by construction.

The sharpest instance is the example the original specification was written around, which appears in the first study's zero-entry arm with an empty violations array:

I don't see anything from that stretch — the entry from March 12 shows a spike in missed classes — 14 days total — and the average delay between notes is now 21 days.

No journal, no entry, no spike, no averages, and no violation. The specification's motivating example is invisible to the suite the specification produced.

This is worth separating from the model's behaviour: turn classification is a deterministic Swift function over the message text, not a model output. Both distributions are degenerate, and both are code defects. That correction never fires — in a study whose cast includes a persona briefed to misremember and correct itself, and whose opening prompts include a flat contradiction of an earlier claim — is a classifier problem with a deterministic fix.

4.6 Persona sensitivity

In Study I three of ten personas were worse with a journal. In Study II all ten are, and the sign reverses for seven. The persona the eight-entry journal helped most is the one the nine-month journal hurts most.

We read this as a property of the failure rather than of the personas. Quoting a person's own words back at them does not depend on who the person is, so a defect that can fire on any conversational turn distributes evenly across the cast — whereas the first study's pattern, in which the personas that interrogate the archive fared worse, is what a retrieval-quality effect looks like.

4.7 Cost

A thirty-two-fold increase in corpus size costs roughly a doubling of median latency, so the context budget is bounding prompt growth as intended. The tail is the product concern: p90 and maximum latency on the seeded arm are several times the median, and no condition in either study exceeds 262 entries, which is small for a journal of nine months' standing.

5 Discussion: reference, not reasoning

The failures reported above look heterogeneous — invented quotations, invented dates, invented counts — and have historically been treated as separate defects with separate checks. We propose they are one failure with three surfaces.

In each case the model is asked to emit a token sequence whose correctness is determined by whether it matches something in an external store. Nothing in the architecture of a 3B-parameter decoder provides a mechanism for that check. The model produces text that is plausible given its context, and plausible journal text is by construction indistinguishable from real journal text; plausible dates are indistinguishable from real dates. The model is not reasoning badly. It is doing exactly what it does, on a task that requires a capability it does not have.

This reframing has predictive content, and §4.3 is the test. If these were reasoning failures, supplying better information — a date anchor, a clearer instruction — would reduce them. It did not; the target failure became more frequent. If they are reference failures, then removing the opportunity to emit an unverified reference should eliminate them. It did, completely, in the one case where that was done.

It also explains the otherwise puzzling shape of Table 2. The zero-entry arm is now nearly perfect not because the model improved but because there is nothing to refer to: a condition with no corpus removes the opportunity for reference failure almost entirely. The seeded arm is worse not because a journal degrades the model but because a journal is what makes reference possible at all. The measured difference between the arms is very largely a measure of opportunity, not of competence.

6 Implications for the product

The existing principle — code decides, the model renders — draws the boundary in the right place but one step too late. Rendering a reference is a decision, and it is the decision the model cannot execute. We propose extending the principle so that the model never emits a reference, only prose and reference tokens.

  1. Quotations become tokens. To quote entry 3 the model emits a marker; the renderer substitutes the verbatim text from the store. A fabricated quotation becomes structurally impossible rather than something detected afterwards, and the grounding scorer can be deleted rather than tuned.
  2. Dates become tokens. The model never writes a date; it emits a marker and the renderer formats the entry's own timestamp. This is the same move already made for counts, which is the one reference class the product currently gets right.
  3. Unmarked emphasis is stripped at render. Any italic span not backed by a token is rendered as plain text, so the typographic contract is enforced by the renderer instead of being asked of the model on every turn.
  4. Turn classification is a code defect and should be fixed as one. Two degenerate distributions and three permanently silent cases are not model behaviour and do not need a model to repair.

The division of labour this implies is unusually clear for a product of this kind. On the evidence here the model is a capable conversational surface — scaffolding escapes into user-visible text twice in 3,386 turns, safety routing behaves as specified, and tone holds across forty-message sessions. It is an unreliable index. The product should use it as the voice and never as the record.

7 Threats to validity

  • The principal comparison is confounded. Study II changed the journal and the pipeline together. Only the zero-entry arm isolates the pipeline and only Study II's two arms isolate the journal; the eight-entry arm crosses both axes and supports neither comparison.
  • One seeded world. The nine-month corpus is a single authored persona. No claim here separates model behaviour from properties of that particular corpus, and the counterfactual world-pairs that would do so are not implemented.
  • Both interlocutors are synthetic. The person is a persona-briefed instance of the same model. Designed refusals and fallback lines in the data are properties of the harness, not of the application.
  • One instrumented field is assumed, not measured. Evidence state in Study II is set from whether the arm has entries rather than from what retrieval returned, so it restates the condition and carries no information. It is reported here for completeness and used for nothing.
  • One historical figure is not recoverable. The first study's fabricated-quotation count is a replay figure, obtained by re-running the repaired scorer over archived bodies; the artifact itself records zero, because the scorer could not execute when the run was made.
  • A published comparison in the original specification is inconsistent. Its two rates are computed on different bases; recomputed consistently the contrast is wider than published, so the specification's argument survives its own arithmetic error.
  • The date measure is textual. Month-day assertions are counted by pattern over reply bodies. It does not detect relative-date errors ("last Tuesday"), so Table 4 is a lower bound.

8 Conclusion

A 3B-parameter on-device model is an adequate conversational surface for a journalling application and an inadequate one for any claim about the journal's contents. The distinction is not one of degree that better prompting narrows; the one controlled comparison available here shows prompting moving the wrong way while a structural change eliminated its target outright.

The corresponding product move is to make unverifiable references inexpressible rather than detectable — to route every quotation, date and count through a token the renderer resolves against the store. The evaluation consequence is that the most expensive check in the suite becomes unnecessary, which is a better outcome than the threshold it is currently waiting for.
