The model points and Swift quotes: a pre-registered replication that refuted its own hypothesis

Six hundred long-form conversations across three pipelines. Fabricated quotation on a nine-month journal fell from 56.0% of turns to zero — and the mechanism was not the one the previous report predicted.

Apple on-device foundation model (~3B), iOS 27 Seven predictions registered at 1689c22, before generation

Abstract

1 Introduction

A previous report on this system argued that the grounding failures of a ~3B on-device model — invented quotations, invented dates, an inability to count — are one failure rather than three: the model cannot verify a reference against an external store, and no amount of prompting supplies the missing mechanism. It recommended making unverifiable references inexpressible rather than detectable, by routing every quotation and date through a token that a renderer resolves against the store.

That recommendation was implemented as specification 050. The model now emits {{quote:N}} and {{date:N}} markers against an evidence pack built by Swift, and a single renderer expands them, strips unbacked emphasis and drops quotations nothing supports. The italic-quote contract the previous scorer depended on was retired outright.

This is the replication. The design is identical to the previous study — same arms, same corpus, same seeds, same scorers — and the pipeline is the only variable, which makes it the first clean single-variable comparison in the series. Seven predictions were committed before any text was generated. Five were met. Two were falsified, and both falsifications land on the previous report rather than on the product.

2 Method

Each arm runs 100 conversations of twenty to fifty messages, both sides generated on-device, with the assistant going through the production path. The unit of analysis is the generated turn: an assistant row the model actually wrote, excluding error rows and the Swift-computed statistics path. All rates are gating-only, matching the production gating function.

Because specification 050 retires italics as the quote vehicle and the renderer strips unmarked italics, the fabricated-quotation scorer loses its vehicle whether or not the model improved. Reporting that drop alone would repeat the error that began this series — an absence of violations read as quality. The study therefore measures two levels that are never merged.

3.2 The model result, and why it refutes the hypothesis

Prediction P4 was the load-bearing one. If reference failure is structural, the model should have kept emitting unbacked quotations and raw italics for the renderer to remove, on at least a quarter of seeded turns. It did so on a fortieth of them.

Falsified — P4

The previous report treated the limit as architectural. On this evidence it was substantially a matter of what the prompt asked for: told to point rather than to italicise, the model largely stopped reaching for the old vehicle. Its §5 overstated the case and should be amended.

That conclusion needs one qualification, and it cuts the other way. The model is not reliably pointing either: of the seeded turns that had matched evidence to point at, only expanded a quote marker, and markers resolved to nothing. The turns that did not point did not fabricate; they paraphrased in second person, which ask-core@19 permits. The product has traded fabrication for vagueness. That is a good trade and not a free one, and no scorer in the suite measures its cost.

3.3 A failure mode neither earlier study saw

The marker grammar gave the model new tokens, and some of them reach the screen.

From a product standpoint this is worse than a fabricated quotation: a fabricated quote reads as prose, whereas the prompt's own furniture on screen reads as a broken application. The renderer is specified as the single choke point for Ask bodies and did not catch these. It is also the most likely contributor to the one regression below.

3.4 The regression

Prediction P2 asked only that the zero-entry arm not get worse. It did, slightly, on a complete arm of generated turns.

3.5 Dates — a finding this report withdrew

This section first reported that dates moved sharply the wrong way: seeded-arm assertions rose from 118 to 290, with 250 passing every check. That counted assertions, not errors, and the instrument to tell the two apart did not exist when it was written.

It does now. A scorer added afterwards fires when a reply names a month and day that no cited entry carries. Replayed over both archives, unbacked dates on the seeded arm went 3.9% → 1.4%; on the zero-entry arm, where every asserted date is invented by construction, 0.5% → 0.1%. The evidence pack improved date grounding by more than half. The assertion count rose because the date marker made the model date things more often, and a far higher share of those dates are correct by construction — the renderer expands them from the entry's own timestamp.

Measuring salience and calling it error is the same mistake as reading an absence of violations as quality, pointed the other way. P3, which covered the zero-entry arm, was met and stays met.

Corrected after publication

Unbacked dates, scored against the citations each reply carries: seeded 63 of 1,626 (3.9%) → 24 of 1,661 (1.4%); zero-entry 9 (0.5%) → 1 (0.1%). The direction of this finding is the opposite of what this report first published.

The instrument gap this section originally identified was real, and closing it is what produced the correction: a reference class with no check is not a quiet one, it is an unmeasured one, and it can be improving or degrading without anybody knowing which.

3.6 Routing and cost

4 How the output changed

The rates in §3 say how often a check fired. They do not say what the assistant actually sounded like, and across three pipelines it changed more than the violation counts alone suggest. Reply shape below is measured from the text in every study by the same patterns, not read from recorded fields — those arrived over three runs and the first has none of them.

4.1 The same question, three pipelines

Run identifiers are seeded identically in every study, so the first turn of run 000 is the same persona asking the same opening question of three different pipelines. It is the only strictly paired comparison the series has — every later turn depends on what the assistant said before it.

The zero-entry column is the cleanest progression in the series: a reply that invents notebook structure over an empty archive, then one that simply says it cannot see anything, then the same with the reason made explicit. The seeded column shows the trade this report is about. Study I hedges — it denies, then quotes anyway. Study II commits fully to the italic contract and quotes a whole entry as a heading. Study III neither hedges nor quotes: it paraphrases, and the one piece of hard evidence it offers is a date the renderer expanded from a marker.

That date also shows the seam. "In April 26, 2026, you spoke of…" is not a sentence a person would write; the renderer substituted a formatted date into glue the model had built for a different shape. The reference is now correct and the prose around it is not yet.

6 Discussion

Three studies now support one claim without qualification: every time a decision has been taken away from this model, quality has improved, and the improvements have been large. Moving channel selection into code took journal voice on an empty archive from 36.8% to zero. Moving quotation into a renderer took invented material on a nine-month journal from 56.6% to 2.0%. Nothing else attempted in this series has produced movement of that size.

What this replication removes is the explanation the previous report attached to that claim. The structural account predicted the model would keep producing unverifiable references and be restrained only by the renderer; instead it largely stopped producing them once asked for markers. The mechanism was not the model's inability to check a reference but the contract it was given. Italics mean emphasis everywhere in its training distribution, and a rule asking it to mean something else on every turn was the defect — not the parameter count.

The practical consequence is narrower than the previous recommendation and better supported. It is not that small models cannot refer; it is that a reference contract must be expressed in a vocabulary the model does not already use for something else. {{quote:N}} works because nothing else in the distribution claims that token. Italics failed because everything does.

Two costs are now visible that were not before. The system has become more reticent: it quotes far less than the evidence available to it would support, and paraphrase is not measured by any check in the suite. And the marker vocabulary is itself leaking onto the screen. Both are consequences of the same change, and neither would appear in a report that led with the headline number.

7 Threats to validity

  • The fabricated-quotation scorer does not measure the same thing in both studies. Its vehicle was retired between them. The drop to zero is reported as two measurements, never as a single trend line, and P1 was pre-registered as nearly guaranteed for this reason.
  • Model-level evidence is counters, not text. AskResult carries only the rendered body, so what the model emitted before rendering is known through ReplyRenderStats and not by scoring it directly.
  • The whole change set moved, not just the renderer. ask-core@17 to @19 includes prompt rewrites, the evidence pack and the ladder copy; no claim here isolates the renderer from the prompt.
  • Conversations are not paired past the first turn. The person's turns are generated from the assistant's replies, so a changed assistant produces a changed conversation. Only distributions are comparable.
  • One authored seeded world, both interlocutors synthetic. Unchanged from the earlier studies, and unchanged in its consequences.
  • Paraphrase is unmeasured. The central new cost identified in §4 has no check. It is asserted from the renderer counters and from reading turns, not quantified.

8 Conclusion

Specification 050 did what it was designed to do, by a wide margin, and got slightly faster doing it. The recommendation that produced it was right about what to build and wrong about why it would work.

The corrected account is more useful than the one it replaces. A small model can be held to a reference contract, provided the contract does not ask it to overload a token the language already uses for something else. What remains open is the cost of the trade — a system that quotes a third as often as it has evidence for, and a marker vocabulary that has begun appearing in user-visible text. Neither is measured by anything currently in the suite, which is where the next study should start.
